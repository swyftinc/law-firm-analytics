# The interview

Three questions at most, asked only when the answer is not already clear from the conversation, a saved profile, or the files. The point is to get to a useful chart or memo fast. People who open this have a Clio export and twenty minutes.

Never ask what kinds of charts the person likes. The skill has the taste. After the first output, ask once whether they want anything different, and remember the answer.

## Before asking anything

1. Look for a saved profile: a `firm-profile.md` in the working folder, a profile block pasted into the conversation, or project instructions that contain one. If it answers the questions, use it and say so in one line: "Using your saved profile: owner, Clio, Monday partner meeting."
2. Look at what was attached. A Clio realization report tells you the system and the billing model before you ask.
3. Look at the request. "Show me where we're losing money on ads" already names the decision.

## The three questions

Ask them together in one short message, not one at a time. Offer a default for each so a one word reply works.

**1. Who is this for?** "Are you the owner or managing partner, an ops or office lead, or a lawyer looking at your own matters?" The answer sets the default focus and the register.

| Role | Leads with | Reads at |
|---|---|---|
| Owner or managing partner | Money and decisions: lead conversion and cost, realization, collections, receivables, revenue by practice area | Monday, before the partner meeting |
| Ops or office lead | Flow and capacity: lead volume and speed, matters per lawyer, cycle time, stalled work, billing lag | Weekly, to run the office |
| Lawyer | Their own matters: time, billing, collections, aging | Whenever, usually month end |

**2. Where do your records live?** "Where do you keep matters, time and billing? Clio, MyCase, Filevine, a spreadsheet, something else? And do you have a marketing spend sheet or an intake call log?" This starts the data path in `connect-your-data.md`.

**3. What is the decision?** "What is this for: a meeting, a decision you are facing, or a number someone keeps asking about?" The answer picks the first chart or the lede of the memo.

If the person gives a one word answer to all three, go. If something is ambiguous, ask one follow up, not a questionnaire.

## What to infer, not ask

- **Billing model:** read it from the data (a time and billing report means hourly; flat fees in a matter list; settlements) or from the practice areas. Ask only if the data cannot say.
- **Which metrics:** propose the two or three that fit the role and decision, say why in one sentence, and proceed. "I'll start with cost per signed matter by source and speed to lead, because you said you are deciding where to put ad dollars." Do not ask the person to pick metrics from a list.
- **Window and comparison:** use the defaults in `metrics.md`.
- **Chart type:** from the question, per `chart-doctrine.md`.

## After the first output

Ask one line: "Anything you'd change? Say 'more like this' or 'less like this' about anything you saw." Record the answer in the profile. Typical notes: wants dollars not percentages, wants fewer charts, wants names left out, wants a brand color.

## Brand color

If the person names a brand color, use it as the accent after checking it has at least 4.5:1 contrast on white. If it does not, pick the darkest shade of that hue that does and say so. Otherwise use the default. Never ask about color up front.

## The profile

Save what you learned in `firm-profile.md` (see `profile-template.md`) so the next run does not start from zero.

- **Claude Code:** write the file in the working folder and tell the person where it is.
- **claude.ai or the API:** you cannot write to their account, so print the profile as a short text block and say: "Paste this into your project's instructions (or the start of a new chat) and I will skip these questions next time."

Keep the profile to what helps next time. Do not store client names, matter details, or any numbers.
